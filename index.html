Prototype context Background & design rationale — personal reporting widgets ▾
What this screen does
Places a row of personal performance widgets at the top of the logged-in user's dashboard, directly above their existing work lists. The row is fixed to My work — the applications allocated to the logged-in user — and carries no scope control. Team and whole-of-council figures live on the Applications page. The widget set, card anatomy and comparison rules are otherwise identical to that page; widget 12 (Awaiting RFI response) is exclusive to this screen. Everything below the widget row is the existing dashboard: the Admin, Active, Determine and Completed tabs and their grouped work queues.
Why designed this way
When Build AU becomes the primary assessment system, councils lose the performance reporting currently embedded in their ERPs (Pathway, TechOne, Authority). Foundation partner research (Canada Bay, North Sydney, Ryde, Hornsby) confirmed performance reporting is a baseline expectation rather than a differentiator. Individual planners asked for their own numbers in the place they already start their day, so the personal widgets sit on the dashboard rather than in a separate reporting area. Whole-of-council figures have moved to the Applications page, where the whole-of-council data set already lives.
Audience
Every user with an assessment caseload — Senior Statutory Planners, assessing officers, and team leaders looking at their own work rather than the team's. The widgets always describe the logged-in user, so no persona switch or role check is needed. A team leader who wants her team's numbers goes to the Applications page and selects the team there; the dashboard stays personal.
Design decisions
Widget selection, definitions and comparison behaviour follow the BASS: Performance Reporting — High-level Stories widget array. Ten widgets appear here: nine shared with the Applications page plus widget 12. Widget 5 is excluded per the spec as too close to widget 4. Widgets 7, 8, 10 and 11 are drawn with a dashed border and a "Later phase" tag — 7 until team performance periods are implemented, 8 until the planning-reform application types settle, and 10 and 11 together because both depend on stop-the-clock tracking. That leaves six widgets in Phase 1 here: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6 and 12, and they are ordered first so the live set reads before the deferred set. Counts (1, 2, 3) compare against the previous calendar month. Duration widgets state the target only — Target: 95 days — resolving the earlier inconsistency where widget 9 also stated its variance; the configuration variable names are internal and do not surface on the card. Whether the target is being met is carried by the colour of the value: red over target, amber on it, green under. Widget 6 shows two tiers — applications already overdue in red, and beneath them the count due to pass the target within seven days in amber — so a coordinator sees what is about to go wrong as well as what already has. Cards here carry no scope chip: the row is fixed to the logged-in user, so a chip could only ever read My work and the row heading already says it. On the Applications page, where scope varies, every card carries one. Durations are whole days. Averages built on fewer than five applications carry a low-volume marker. Cards are compact and carousel-paged (four visible of ten) so the work queue below stays reachable. Reference numbers are shown for review only and would not ship in the product.
Open questions
1. Six Phase 1 widgets and four deferred — is that the right split, and should the deferred ones appear at all in the shipped product? 2. Should the row load expanded or collapsed? 3. Widget 12 has been renamed from RFI letters in progress to Awaiting RFI response. That changes the population, not just the wording: letters being drafted are a different set from letters already sent and waiting on the applicant. It also overlaps with widget 11 (Clock stopped), since in NSW the stopped-clock period is usually triggered by the RFI being sent. Is that overlap acceptable, or does one of the two go the way of widget 5? 4. Scope selection has been deliberately kept off this screen, so a team leader must change page to read her team's numbers. Is that the right separation, or does she want My work and My team side by side where she starts her day? 5. With variance lines removed, a duration card signals pass or fail only by the colour of the number — is that enough, or should the wording return? 6. Widget 9 is Applications-only, so the vetting target does not appear here — correct, or should a planner see their own lodgement turnaround?

Prototype context Background & design rationale — whole-of-council reporting widgets ▾
What this screen does
Adds a row of reporting widgets to the top of the Applications page, above the existing application list. The row opens on Whole of council and a scope control switches the same widgets to any single planning team. Scope selection lives only here — the user dashboard row is fixed to the logged-in user's own work. The widget set, card anatomy and comparison rules are shared with that screen; widget 9 (Avg lodgement days) is exclusive to this one. A data export section extracts assessment and referral data for use in external reporting tools such as Power BI.
Why designed this way
These widgets began as a separate "planning leadership" dashboard. They have been folded into the Applications page instead, because that page already holds the whole-of-council data set the widgets describe — a leader reading a council-wide number and then wanting the rows behind it should not have to change screens. Personal caseload widgets remain on the user dashboard. Foundation partner research (Canada Bay, North Sydney, Ryde, Hornsby) confirmed councils need to monitor assessment speed in real time against tightening targets: 95 days for FY 26–27, reducing to 85 days from 1 July 2027. Whole-of-council alone was not enough — a coordinator runs a team, not a council, and without team scope she has to export to Excel to see her own numbers, which is the behaviour Build AU is meant to replace.
Audience
Any user with access to the Applications page. The widgets are not framed around a leadership persona — they are page-level statistics describing whichever scope the user selects. Any user may select any team, consistent with whole-of-council performance already being open to everyone who can see the page.
Design decisions
Widget selection, definitions and comparison behaviour follow the BASS: Performance Reporting — High-level Stories widget array. Ten widgets appear here: nine shared with the user dashboard plus widget 9. Widget 5 is excluded per the spec. Widgets 7, 8, 10 and 11 are tagged "Later phase" — 10 and 11 together because both depend on stop-the-clock tracking, which is not captured in Phase 1. That leaves six widgets in Phase 1 here: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6 and 9, ordered first so the live set reads before the deferred set. Counts (1, 2, 3) compare against the previous calendar month for the selected scope. Duration widgets state the target only — Target: 95 days — which resolves the earlier inconsistency where widget 9 also stated its variance; the configuration variable names are internal and no longer surface on the card. Widget 6 carries two tiers instead of a target: the count already past the target, and beneath it the count that will pass it within the next seven days. The warning window is relative to the target, so it follows the target down as it steps 95 to 85 — unlike the earlier at-risk design, which hardcoded 80 days and would have gone stale. Targets stay council-level at every scope, because the Statement of Expectations is a council commitment, not a team KPI. Scope selection is a dropdown rather than a chip row, since councils run anywhere from two to a dozen planning teams. When a team is selected the row takes an accent bar, a tinted header and a per-card scope chip. Team is taken from each application's currently assigned team, and applies to unallocated work too, so every widget can be team-scoped. Team figures sum exactly to the council figures; averages are weighted by application count, not the mean of team means. Durations are whole days, and averages built on fewer than five applications carry a low-volume marker. Menus carry no explanatory prose — team name and count only. Every displayed value must be reachable via API, with scope and teamId parameters, so the same figures can be pulled into Power BI. The widgets do not follow the list filters. The export sits below the application list and produces two flat CSV tables rather than one joined table, matching the structure councils need for Power BI ingestion.
Open questions
1. The widgets do not follow the list filters, and now there are two scope mechanisms on one screen — a user can read "48 active" for a team above a list showing three filtered rows. The accent bar and card chips say which scope is active but not that the list is independent of it. Is that acceptable, or should the scope menu offer an Apply to list action? 2. Should the row also appear on the Completed tab, where several metrics no longer apply? 3. Where do councils set the target values, and who is allowed to change them? Targets are revised each July, so they need to be effective-dated rather than a single stored number someone remembers to change. 4. Counts compare to the previous calendar month, so early in a month widgets 2 and 3 will always look down — is a rolling comparison better? 5. Whole days breaks the vetting metric: widget 9 has a 7-day target, so a team averaging 7.4 days displays as 7 and reads compliant while failing. Major Projects shows exactly that. Should widget 9 carry one decimal? 6. Scope resolves to the application's current team, so reassigning an application rewrites last month's figures for both teams. Team figures are not point-in-time reproducible — acceptable, or do councils need month-end snapshots? 7. Should the row remember the last team used, or always open on whole of council? 8. Is an Unallocated applications widget wanted at team scope? Allocation is the coordinator's core job and it is buildable from data that exists today. 9. The count beside each team in the picker is unlabelled — accept it as learnable, label the group, or drop the counts?
